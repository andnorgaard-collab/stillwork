Project lead · Holdånd · a story in four courses

From soil to table.

Take a class of new gastronomy students who hardly know each other. Give them a field, a kitchen and two of the best chefs in Copenhagen. Ask for one dinner. What you get back is a team.

  1. The field
  2. The fire
  3. The pass
  4. The table
01

Dirt first.

Before anyone touched a knife, we went to the field. Pumpkins under the leaves, potatoes in the ground, kale up to the knees and a grower with a lot to tell. It is hard to stay a stranger to someone you have dug potatoes with.

02

Then fire.

Back in the kitchen, Mette Søberg and Jonathan Knoll took over. Charcoal, koji and a lot of patience. They showed the students what a few good techniques can do with what we had just pulled out of the ground.

03

Many hands, one pass.

Plating is a team sport. Every bowl goes through several pairs of hands, and nothing leaves the counter until everyone's part is on it. Students and chefs side by side, same counter, same deadline.

04

And then the table.

The day ended where it should: at the table, with food the students had harvested, cooked and served themselves. Proud is the right word.

Behind it

People together, around a purpose.

Holdånd is Danish for team spirit. New students drop out when they feel alone, so the idea was to give them a reason to belong in their very first weeks: a shared job, from the soil to the table.

I had the idea and ran the project, with EU funding, a lot of phone calls and very generous partners. The rest was the students.
